## Abstract

Class imbalance remains a critical challenge in machine learning, especially in high-dimensional settings where minority instances are often dominated by the majority class. This leads to biased models and reduced predictive performance. Existing undersampling methods may fail to preserve important information and can discard critical patterns during sample selection. 
This paper proposes a novel undersampling framework based on informative latent mapping and autoencoder-driven feature extraction. By maximizing mutual information between the data and latent spaces, the method learns compact and informative representations. This reduces dimensionality, improves computational efficiency, and mitigates the curse of dimensionality while enhancing generalization. 
Support points are then applied in the latent space to select a representative subset of the majority class. These points are mapped back to the original space to construct a balanced dataset that retains key structural information. Experimental results on multiple binary classification tasks demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms several state-of-the-art undersampling approaches, achieving improved predictive performance and better class balance.
